Ask about content care

A useful publishing rhythm, without the content mill.

An article earns attention for years if it is specific, honest, and technically reachable. All 3 conditions are part of the job.

Written from your expertise

Drafts start with what you actually know: the questions clients ask, the decisions you walk them through, the mistakes you see. You review every word before it publishes. For regulated firms, your compliance reviewer stays in the workflow.

Published as real pages

Each article gets its own URL on your domain with Article schema, internal links, and the full text present in the raw HTML. Some blog widgets inject their article text only after JavaScript runs. If a crawler does not render that script, it never sees the copy. I have audited live blogs where the visible articles were missing from the raw HTML.

Submitted the same day

On publish, the sitemap updates and the URL is submitted through IndexNow to participating search engines. Search Console and Bing records are then reviewed for discovery and indexing status where available. Submission confirms only that the URL was received, not that it was crawled or indexed.

A cadence that holds up

I would rather publish 1 to 2 useful articles than 30 generated ones. Google's spam policy focuses on large amounts of unoriginal content created to manipulate rankings, no matter how it is produced. Volume is not the product here. Usefulness is.

Sources get quoted. Become one.

Assistants and search engines need a source before they can surface or cite it. TMN has received an advisor inquiry after ChatGPT surfaced the studio. The buyer still needed a real site with specific work, clear service pages, and something worth checking.

“Can I see something you have written about this?”A question your website should be able to answer.

I cannot make anyone read it.

Publishing well improves what machines and buyers can find, parse, and verify. It does not command rankings, citations, or traffic, and anyone promising those is selling something they do not control.

What is never promised

What each article ships with

  • Built

    A real URL on your domain with the full text in the raw HTML, so crawlers can read it without executing JavaScript.

  • Marked up

    Article schema with headline, dates, and your practice as author and publisher, plus internal links to the service pages the article supports.

  • Submitted

    Sitemap and IndexNow on publish day, followed by Search Console and Bing review where the client account provides access. A received submission does not prove crawling or indexing.

  • Reported

    A short monthly note covering what published, what got indexed, and what the next topics are, in plain English.

What I will not sell you

Autopilot volume. Tools now write and post daily without a human reading a word. Google's spam policies call the mass-produced version scaled content abuse, and a machine-written pile with no editor is also just bad marketing. Every article here is researched, edited with you, and approved before it ships.

Compliance review is part of the workflow.

Advisory, healthcare, and legal content carries claim rules. Drafts are prepared for your reviewer before publish, required disclosures go where your reviewer places them, and approved copy comes from your firm. I do not give compliance advice.

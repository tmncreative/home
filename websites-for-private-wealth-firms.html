A principal's commissioning brief.

A strong private wealth website starts by resolving the firm's real trust decisions, not by choosing a template.

The firm and the client fit

Define who the firm serves, which relationships it handles best, and what makes the client experience materially different.

The people behind the advice

Give each principal and advisor enough space for credentials, role, experience, and a plain explanation of how the team works together.

The service model

Explain planning, investment management, family coordination, and other services at the level the firm can support and approve.

The next responsible step

Route qualified prospects to a conversation while keeping sensitive client information inside approved portals and providers.

One site, several trust decisions.

Private wealth sites rarely serve one reader. The structure should help each audience find the evidence it needs without turning the homepage into a directory.

Prospective clients
Who the firm serves, what the relationship covers, who they will work with, and how to begin.
Family members
A clear explanation of the team, continuity, communication, and how the firm supports more than one generation.
Attorneys and CPAs
Enough specificity to understand the firm's role, coordination model, and professional fit before making a referral.
Prospective hires
The firm's point of view, leadership, client standards, and the kind of practice the team is building.

Build for the firm's review process.

Review responsibilities should be settled before copy and design become expensive to change. TMN prepares the website for the client's reviewers and keeps approval with the firm.

TMN prepares

  • Factual positioning and service copy within the approved scope
  • Team bios, disclosure locations, link labels, and review-ready layouts
  • Semantic HTML, search foundations, internal links, and matching structured data
  • A clear revision path for firm, counsel, CCO, or broker-dealer comments

The firm approves

  • Services, credentials, affiliations, and every factual statement about the firm
  • Testimonials, endorsements, performance presentation, and required disclosures
  • Form ADV, Form CRS, BrokerCheck, privacy, and other filing or link requirements when applicable
  • Archiving, recordkeeping, supervision, legal interpretation, and final publication

TMN does not provide investment advice or legal or compliance approval. For a deeper planning resource, use the RIA website compliance guide and have the firm's own reviewers interpret the rules that apply.

Private wealth website questions.

The practical details firms ask before commissioning the work.

Do you build only for registered investment advisers?

No. TMN also works with boutique wealth managers, multi-family offices, single-family-office teams, family-office-facing advisors, and other professional firms. The engagement must fit TMN's published scope and the client's own legal and compliance requirements.

Can TMN approve financial-services compliance?

No. TMN can prepare factual copy, disclosure locations, and page layouts for the firm's review. The client's CCO, broker-dealer, counsel, or other responsible reviewer interprets the applicable rules and gives final approval.

How much does a private wealth website cost?

Most private wealth and family-office-facing projects fit TMN's Custom Studio Build from $5,000. A smaller Full Site Rebuild may fit from $3,750 when the approved scope is narrower. The signed agreement controls the final scope and price.

How quickly will we see the first design?

Custom Studio Build projects typically target a first review within 7 to 10 days after kickoff. Final launch timing depends on firm-supplied content, integrations, legal or compliance review, revisions, and approvals.

Who owns the completed website?

After full payment, clients receive the completed website files and project assets created by TMN, subject to the signed agreement. Domains, third-party accounts, licensed assets, hosting, and integrations remain governed by their providers and separate terms.

Can the site connect to a client portal or scheduler?

Yes, when the provider supports the connection and it is included in scope. Secure client data should remain inside the firm's approved portal or provider. TMN does not move sensitive client data into a public website form.

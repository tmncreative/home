A 14-year practice, described by a template.

Don Hilario founded the firm in 2012 and has worked with Google employees since 2008. The practice is specific: RSU vest schedules, concentrated employer stock, the tax and retirement decisions that follow. The previous site came from a subscription advisor platform. It could describe that specialty. It could not show it.

Found through ChatGPT

Don asked an AI assistant for a new website with financial-advisor infrastructure, then narrowed the shortlist on speed, support, RIA fluency, and boutique service. His summary of the final comparison, in his words: the platform option was “much more of a platform,” and TMN was “much more of a boutique design relationship.”

Compliance is part of the build

The firm is an SEC-registered adviser. Don's compliance consultant reviewed the preview, supplied the required disclosures, and confirmed the language for each planning illustration. The firm kept final approval of every page. TMN does not approve or certify advertising for any adviser.

29 articles with URLs to keep

The firm had 29 published insights on the old platform. A redesign that broke those addresses would throw away 14 years of writing and the search history attached to it.

The founder publishes

Don writes practice updates himself. A site he could not update without a vendor in the loop would have recreated the platform dependency the redesign was meant to end.

What TMN built into the site.

The answer to a specialty that a template could only describe was to show it. The site draws the decisions a technology professional actually faces, states the firm's role in its own words, and keeps everything the firm had already published.

  • Planning illustrations, disclosedAn RSU grant mapped across 16 vest dates, a concentration slider that shows what the rest of a portfolio has to do around one large position, a bond ladder and retirement income timeline with a Roth window, and a coverage gap graphic. Each is labeled as an illustration and carries the hypothetical-example disclosure the compliance consultant confirmed.
  • Wealth management firstThe first preview led with planning. Don corrected the emphasis: more than 90% of the practice is wealth management with planning included, so the site was rebuilt around that, with planning-only and flat-fee engagement paths kept visible.
  • The firm's words, verbatimDon's 2 sentences describing what the firm is responsible for went in as written. The compliance consultant's 3 disclosures went in exactly as written. TMN drafted around the firm's language rather than over it.
  • 29 articles migrated, addresses preservedEvery published insight moved to the new site at its original URL, with an Insights search and 8 topic pages added so the archive is browsable rather than buried.
  • A founder publishing portalA private portal at the firm's own address lets Don publish updates without a vendor in the loop. The firm's standing disclosure is appended to every post automatically. Don published his first update himself on Sep 1, the day after launch. The firm continues on month-to-month site care with the managed publishing portal.
  • Search foundation on day 1Google Search Console and Bing verified, a 53-URL sitemap submitted, redirects and canonicals in place, and structured data for the firm, the founder, 8 services, and all 29 articles. The RIA website compliance guide covers the disclosure structure.
  • Hand-coded, no page builderStatic pages on the firm's domain with 1 third-party script, a privacy-respecting analytics tag. Anyone can confirm that by viewing the source.

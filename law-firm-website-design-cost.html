The Ranges

What a firm website actually costs.

US market, 2026, for firms from solo practitioners to mid-size. Where TMN publishes a fixed price, it is stated exactly.

01

Template legal platform · $200 to $500 per month

The big legal directory and marketing platforms will build you a site inside their system. It is fast and it works, but you are renting: the site is theirs, the design is shared with other firms in your market, and leaving usually means starting over. Over 3 years that is $7,000 to $18,000 with nothing owned at the end.

02

Freelancer · $1,500 to $6,000 once

Workable for a solo practice if the freelancer has done legal work before. The risk specific to law is compliance: most freelancers have never read your state bar's advertising rules, so review and disclaimers land back on you. Ask directly whether they have built for a regulated profession.

03

Independent studio · $2,250 to $10,000 once

Custom design and build with strategy and copy included, and bar rules handled during the build rather than after. This is TMN's tier: $2,250 Starter Refresh, $3,750 Full Site Rebuild, $5,000+ Custom Studio Build. You own the files afterward.

04

Legal marketing agency · $10,000 to $50,000+

Full agencies bundle the site with ongoing legal SEO and paid search, often on a 12-month contract at $3,000 to $10,000 per month. Correct for firms in a heavy paid-search fight for high-value case types. Overkill for a new or small firm that first needs a credible site it owns.

Why Legal Costs More

What separates a $3,000 quote from a $30,000 one.

A firm site is not a normal business site. These are the legal-specific items that move the number, and the ones worth asking any vendor about by name.

01

Bar advertising compliance

Every state regulates attorney advertising. Rules typically govern naming a responsible lawyer and office location, restricting specialist and expert claims, handling testimonials, and required disclaimers on any results you publish. Building this in costs time. Skipping it moves the risk to your license.

02

Practice area depth

Practice areas are the pages that rank and convert, and each needs real substance rather than a paragraph. A firm with 3 practice areas is a materially smaller build than one with 12, and this is usually the single biggest driver of a legal quote.

03

Who writes the legal copy

Legal content written by someone who does not understand your practice reads like it, and clients notice. If the vendor writes it, that is a real share of the fee. If an attorney at the firm writes it, you save money and the copy is usually better.

04

Results, and their disclaimers

Publishing verdicts or settlements is allowed in most states but nearly always requires a disclaimer that past results do not guarantee a similar outcome. Getting the placement and wording right is part of the build, not an afterthought.

05

Intake and case management

Form-to-CRM routing, e-signature, calendar booking, or a live chat vendor each add integration scope. Name every system you use before asking for a number, because integrations are where quotes drift.

06

New firm versus established

A firm with no verdict history and no reviews needs its credibility built out of verifiable substance instead: bar credentials the reader can check, and plain-language explanations of the law that governs their case. That is a writing problem more than a design problem.

FAQ

What firm owners actually ask.

01

How much does a law firm website cost?

Building one runs $1,500 to $50,000+ depending on route. A solo or small firm site from an independent studio runs $2,250 to $10,000 once, plus roughly $50 to $200 per month to host and maintain. Legal marketing agencies bundling SEO and paid search typically start near $3,000 per month on annual contracts.

02

Why is a law firm website more expensive than a normal business site?

Bar advertising compliance, practice-area depth, and legal copy. A restaurant needs a menu and hours. A firm needs pages that survive a bar review, explain complex practice areas credibly, and handle results disclaimers correctly. That is more writing and more care, and it is where the money goes.

03

Is a $500 per month legal platform site worth it?

It is fast and it functions, and for some firms that is enough. Read the ownership terms first: in most of these plans you never own the site, the design is shared across firms in your market, and cancelling means losing it. At $500 per month you pass the cost of an owned custom build in about 8 months.

04

What does a new firm with no case results need?

Credibility built from things a reader can verify. Bar number and standing they can check against the bar's own directory, plain-language explanations of the deadlines and rules that govern their situation, and a clear consultation path. TMN built The Ramsay Law Firm on exactly that approach.

05

Do I own my law firm website?

With TMN, yes: project files transfer after full payment subject to the signed agreement. With platform vendors, usually no. Ask the question directly and get the answer in writing, because it is the difference between an asset and a subscription.

06

How long does a law firm website take?

TMN targets a first draft roughly 72 hours after kickoff for Starter and Full tiers, and a first review in 7 to 10 days for Custom Studio Builds. Launch timing then depends on your review and approvals, which for firms usually means a compliance read before anything goes live.
